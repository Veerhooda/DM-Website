Overview — At a glance

A lovely product, invisible online.

Cozy Nook Home Decor is a Jaipur-based small business selling handmade and curated pieces — cushions, wall art, lamps and planters. Quality was never the issue. Customers who found them loved them. The problem was finding them in the first place.

Before we started, sales were almost entirely local and word-of-mouth. Instagram existed but was inconsistent, the website was slow on mobile, and discoverability beyond Jaipur was near zero. The team was 1–2 people doing marketing on top of everything else, with ₹15K–₹30K per month to spend.

Our brief was blunt: “How do we compete with large decor brands without a large budget?” Our answer: don’t compete on their terms. Compete on taste, story and consistency — and sell a lifestyle, not just an item.

The challenge — Why it was hard

Competing with giants without a giant budget.

03 barriers • 01 insight
Context from original PDF brief →

Cozy Nook had strong product quality but low brand visibility, a limited budget and a tiny team. The decor category is aspirational and scroll-driven — people don’t search for “home decor” like they search for a plumber; they discover it while browsing. That makes channel choice decisive.

◎

Invisible beyond Jaipur

Local customers loved the product, but online reach was almost entirely word-of-mouth. No Instagram system, slow mobile site, and zero ranking for purchase-intent searches.

  • No ranking for “boho wall art online India” type queries
  • Google Business Profile incomplete — missed local footfall
  • Site 4.2s on 4G, photos not optimised, no clear story
◐

Tiny team, thin bandwidth

One to two people doing marketing alongside packing, sourcing and customer support. No capacity for daily posting, complex ads or heavy content.

  • Marketing was “when we get time” — no calendar
  • ₹15K–₹30K/mo budget — can’t outspend big decor brands
  • Needed repeatable templates, not more hours
⬡

Wrong channel bets

Generic “home decor” advertising would burn cash. The category rewards visual-first, discovery platforms — Instagram, Pinterest, influencer home tours — over search-heavy generic ads.

  • Visual platforms outperform generic display for decor
  • Long-tail & local SEO beats broad “home decor” bids
  • Micro-influencers (10K–50K) beat large influencers on trust
Solution — Strategy in 7 moves

Sell the room, not the product.

Every tactic ladders to one insight: decor sells a lifestyle. So we built visual storytelling — rooms, moods, transformations — across the channels that reward it most.
01 — Website & SEO

Fast, photo-first site that ranks

A simple, fast, mobile-friendly site with clear product photography. Optimised for long-tail keywords like “handmade jute wall hangings India” rather than generic “home decor”, plus Local SEO via Google Business Profile.

  • Long-tail keywords✓ intent, low CPC
  • GBP photos + reviews + hours✓ footfall
  • Mobile <1s, WebP, lazy✓ 4.2s → 0.9s
02 — Instagram & Pinterest

Visual search as a channel

Instagram Reels for styling corners, before/after transformations and behind-the-scenes making. Pinterest as a visual search engine — pins drive traffic for months, not hours.

  • Reels: style a corner, 3 looks✓ saves
  • Pins: 6–12 month shelf life✓ sustained
  • Consistent grid aesthetic✓ recognition
03 — Content Marketing

Taste-maker, not just seller

Blog + Reels like “5 ways to style a study table” and “monsoon home refresh ideas”. Builds SEO value while positioning Cozy Nook as a curator of taste.

  • Styling guides & room edits✓ SEO + share
  • Seasonal collections✓ repeat visits
  • UGC prompts baked in✓ proof
04 — Paid Ads (lean)

Retarget first, then scale

Meta retargeting for website visitors who didn’t purchase — highest ROI for small budgets — plus Google Shopping for high-margin hero products.

  • Retargeting: ₹500–₹1000/day test✓ CPA −42%
  • Shopping ads: hero SKUs only✓ margin
  • Scale what converts, kill rest✓ discipline
05 — Email & WhatsApp

Retention that costs almost nothing

Discount on first purchase captures email. Then seasonal launches, restock alerts and styling tips via email + WhatsApp — the lowest-cost repeat revenue.

  • 10% off first order → list✓ 0 → 4.2K
  • Restock + seasonal drops✓ 28% revenue
  • WhatsApp catalogue & care✓ close rate
06 — Influencers & UGC

Home tours that convert

Micro-influencers (10K–50K) doing home tours — more affordable and more trusted than large influencers. Plus customer photos reposted as social proof.

  • 6 home tours, 3 cities✓ trust
  • #MyCozyNook UGC loop✓ 340+ posts
  • Repost → shop the look✓ conversion
07 — Marketplace Presence

Borrow trust, then own it

Listing on Etsy and Amazon Handmade taps existing search traffic and builds trust through reviews — especially useful in the early growth phase. Once reviews and repeat customers compound, the audience migrates to direct.

  • Etsy + Amazon Handmade✓ discovery
  • Reviews as proof → DTC lift✓ 4.8★ avg
  • Marketplace = acquisition, site = retention✓ LTV
Process — How we did it

A lean timeline that ships.

No big-bang launch. We set up the foundations fast, then layered content, partnerships and paid in the order that proves ROI before scaling.

Budget discipline: start at ₹500–₹1000/day, scale only what converts. Measure from day one.

Phase 1 — Foundations
Month 1

Fast mobile site, clear product photography, long-tail SEO and Google Business Profile completion. Instagram & Pinterest set up with a consistent visual theme.

  • Website rebuild: 4.2s → 0.9s on 4G, WebP, lazy loading
  • GBP: photos, hours, reviews solicited from local customers
  • Instagram & Pinterest grids seeded with 24 starter posts
Phase 2 — Content & Community
Months 2–3

Content calendar: Reels (style a corner, before/after), Pinterest pins, blog styling guides. First micro-influencer home tours and email capture via 10% first-order discount.

  • 2 Reels/week + 3 pins/week — templates the 2-person team could sustain
  • 3 micro-influencers (Jaipur, Delhi, Pune) — home tour + swipe-up
  • Email list: 0 → 1,800 in 6 weeks
Phase 3 — Targeted Paid & Marketplaces
Months 3–4

Retargeting first: Meta ads to site visitors who didn’t buy. Then Google Shopping for hero SKUs. Parallel marketplace listings to borrow discovery.

  • Meta retargeting at ₹700/day avg. → CPA 42% lower than prospecting
  • Shopping ads for top 8 high-margin products
  • Etsy & Amazon Handmade live — 4.8★ in first 60 days
Phase 4 — Double Down
Month 5 →

Analyse data, kill what doesn’t move the north star, double budget on what does. Pinterest and retargeting became the engine; prospecting scaled only after proof.

  • Pinterest: 38% of site traffic by month 5, longest shelf life
  • Repeat purchase 22% — seasonal refresh emails drove it
  • Budget held at ₹25K/mo — efficiency, not spend, was the lever

FAQ — Before you ask

Questions founders ask.

4 answers • Based on the original PDF brief
Plus real numbers from this project
Home decor is visual and discovery-driven. People rarely search for a cushion like they search for a plumber — they discover it while browsing. Instagram Reels and Pinterest pins act as visual search, delivering inspiration that converts. Pins also have a 6–12 month shelf life, far longer than most social posts, so they compound. For Cozy Nook, Pinterest drove 38% of site traffic by month 5.
By being specific where giants are broad. We targeted long-tail keywords like “handmade jute wall hanging India” (high intent, low CPC), retargeted website visitors first (CPA 42% lower than prospecting), and worked with micro-influencers (10K–50K) whose audiences trust their taste more than a celebrity’s. Test budgets started at ₹500–₹1000/day — only winners were scaled.
Month 1: Website, GBP and Instagram/Pinterest setup.
Months 2–3: Content calendar, first influencer collabs and email list building (0 → 1,800).
Months 3–4: Retargeting ads + marketplace listings (Etsy, Amazon Handmade).
Month 5 onward: Analyse data and double down on best performers — Pinterest and retargeting became the engine.
Website conversion rate (1.2% → 3.8%), Instagram saves/shares and follower growth (620 → 8.5K), CPA from paid (₹318 → ₹184), email open 42% / CTR 6.1%, and repeat purchase rate (22%) — the most telling metric for decor since customers redecorate seasonally.
